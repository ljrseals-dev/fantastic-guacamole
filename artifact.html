Talking Voder

The 1939 Bell Labs speaking machine as a score. Type a phrase and it becomes key presses: ten band keys, three stop-consonant keys, a buzz or hiss switch and a pitch line. Edit any cell, then play it.

score

Phrase

Roll rows: ten band keys (top is 2650 to 2950 Hz), then the stop keys P/B, T/D, K/G, then the wrist bar (amber buzz, blue hiss) and the pitch line. Drag across cells to paint or erase.

Play it yourself

Ten band keys, three stop keys, wrist bar, pedal. Computer keys A S D F G H J K L ; for the bands, Z X C for the stops, hold Space for hiss.

Checked and not checked

From the CCRMA page on the Voder: ten fingers set the band gains, three further keys gave transient excitation of selected filters for stop consonants, a wrist bar chose buzz or hiss, a foot pedal set pitch.

Not documented there, so assumed here: which filters each stop key excites, the formant values, the letter rules, all timings. The Voder could not buzz and hiss together, so voiced fricatives are buzz only. Nobody has listened to this.

Measured

Each band key sounds in its own band. Stop bursts land on their cell within a few milliseconds and the T burst is brighter than K, which is brighter than P. The same phrase always makes the same score and the same audio.
